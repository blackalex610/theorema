Anatomy · how the format moved

Three eras, one skeleton

The paper has been restructured twice in ten years. The wrapper changed — the load-bearing numbers did not.

2015 – 2018

Module era

ПЪРВИ МОДУЛ 60′ · 20 tasks
ВТОРИ МОДУЛ 90′ · tasks 21–25
  • Module 1 splits internally: задачи с избираем отговор (1–16) then задачи със свободен отговор (17–20)
  • Shared-stem clusters — one figure serving tasks 14, 15 and 16
  • Item types that later vanish: matching tables, ruler-and-grid construction, fill-the-transformation-steps
2019 – 2023

Part era, 60-minute Part 1

ПЪРВА ЧАСТ 60′ · 17–18 MC + 2–3 structured
ВТОРА ЧАСТ 90′ · 3 tasks (5 in 2019)
  • Multiple choice compressed to 18, then two heavyweight structured items (7 and 8 points) close Part 1
  • The “чертежите не са в мащаб” notice moves inline and starts marking the algebra → geometry boundary
  • 2020 drops time labels entirely (COVID sitting); 2019 still runs five Part 2 tasks
2024 – 2026

Current — and it just moved again

2024–25 · ПЪРВА ЧАСТ 75′ · 20 MC, no structured
2026 · ПЪРВА ЧАСТ 90′ · 14 MC + 7 short answer
  • 2024–25 abolish the structured block: Part 1 is 20 pure multiple-choice items
  • 2026 reverses that — multiple choice cut to 14, a кратък свободен отговор block (15–21) returns, Part 1 stretches to 90′
  • Part 2 renumbers to 22–24; total sitting time hits 180 minutes
Ship-blocker for the rewrite — now resolved

backend/nvo_question_catalog.json defines 23 slots with topics for positions 1–20 multiple-choice plus 21–23 open. That is exactly the 2024–2025 shape. The 2026 paper is 24 slots — 14 MC, 7 short-answer, 3 open — so the catalog could not express the newest real format at all.

Fixed: the blueprint is now data (backend/app/nvo_gen/blueprints.py), both shapes are offered side by side, and a third era costs one dict entry. See docs/nvo-generation.md.

The invariants you can hard-code

Checked against all thirteen papers. These held through both restructurings.

  • Block order is fixed. Part 1 runs an algebra/number/data block, then a geometry block. The transition is physically marked by the notice „Чертежите са само за илюстрация. Те не са начертани в мащаб…” — printed inline, immediately before the first geometry item, in 2019, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025.
  • The boundary drifts, the order doesn't. Geometry starts at position 9 (2019, 2025), 10 (2021, 2024, 2026), 11 (2023) or 12 (2022). Never scattered.
  • Points ramp monotonically within Part 1. 2024 runs 2,2,2,3,3,3,3,3,3,3,4,3,3,4,4,4,4,4,4,4. 2026 runs 2,2,2,3,2,3,2,3,3,2,2,3,3,3 for MC then 4,4,5,4,5,4,4 for short answer.
  • Answer letters sit near-uniform. 2026 splits 3/4/4/3 across А/Б/В/Г; 2024 splits 4/5/6/5. 2022 is the outlier at 3/8/4/3. Target uniform, tolerate ±2.
  • Part 2 is always the same three genres: one multi-part algebra item, one word problem (motion, work, mixture or percent), one geometry proof. In that order, in every paper from 2020 on.

What the matrix tells the generator

  • Position 1 is the same item every year. A short arithmetic expression with a sign trap — a negative multiplier, a mixed number, or an order-of-operations bait. Worth 2 points, always.
  • Positions 2–8 are an algebra carousel drawing from nine reusable topics: expression at a value, expand/factor, linear equation, absolute value, inequality → interval, powers, shortcut multiplication, probability, mean.
  • Probability appears exactly once in Part 1, somewhere between position 6 and 9, in all six years.
  • Data reading appears exactly once or twice — a chart or a table — and never disappears. The 2015 ministry directive commits to this explicitly.
  • The geometry block is 5–8 items and always closes with a quadrilateral or a composite figure, never with a plain angle-chase.
  • 3D solids appear at most once per paper (cube, box, pyramid, cone, cylinder), always as a non-metric illustration with dimension labels.

And the ministry published its own design brief. tekst_nvo_bel_math.pdf is the post-2015 MON optimisation memo. For maths it commits to: more routine items in Part 1 testing “устойчивост на знанията”; an algebra-to-geometry ratio of approximately 1 : 1; keeping the chart- and table-reading items; short-answer items built from predominantly independent sub-tasks; and issuing a formula sheet as part of the exam packet. That memo is the generator's requirements document — it should be encoded as validator rules, not prose.
Diagrams · the inventory

Six classes cover every figure in ten years

The specimens below are hand-drawn SVG at the size they'd render in the app — a proof that the ministry's look is reachable with plain primitives and no library.

The licence that makes this tractable: every paper from 2018 on prints „Те не са начертани в мащаб и не са предназначени за директно измерване на дължини и на ъгли.” The figure does not have to be metrically faithful to the numbers in the stem. It has to be topologically right — the correct point between the correct two points, the label on the correct side, the marked angle visibly obtuse if it's obtuse. That collapses the hard problem (constraint-solve an exact diagram) into an easy one (place a canonical shape, put points at parameter t along segments).
Diagrams · the decision

Four ways to handle figures

Today the repo has NvoDiagrams.tsx: twelve hand-written React SVG components, each bound to one specific exam question. It works and it looks right — it just doesn't scale past twelve shapes.

Option 1 — Extend the bespoke-component library

Status quo, scaled

What it is

  • Keep writing one React SVG component per figure shape, growing from 12 to ~50
  • Each takes a typed config; the generator picks the component and fills numbers

For

  • Zero new architecture; the existing twelve keep working
  • Every figure is guaranteed to look right because a human drew it

Against

  • Each new shape costs a developer a day; the content team is blocked on engineering forever
  • Fifty components is thousands of lines of near-duplicate trigonometry
  • Figure and stem live in different files — they can silently drift apart
Use as the fallback, not the plan. It's where you are; it's not where 40+ shapes can live.

Option 2 — Declarative scene spec + one renderer

Recommended

What it is

  • A JSON scene: named points, segments, angle arcs, tick marks, right-angle marks, dashed lines, labels with a side hint
  • One SceneRenderer draws any of it; the item's parameters produce the scene
  • Point positions come from a canonical layout plus a parameter t along a segment — no constraint solver, because the figure needn't be to scale

For

  • A new figure is data, authorable by a maths teacher, reviewable in a diff
  • Deterministic, crisp at any zoom, themeable via currentColor, tiny payload, no runtime dependency
  • One parameter object drives stem, figure and answer — they cannot disagree
  • An aria-label can be generated from the same spec, so the figure is accessible

Against

  • Label collision needs a placement heuristic (8 compass slots, pick the one furthest from any drawn edge)
  • Roughly a week of renderer work before the first figure appears
This is the one to build. It covers classes A, B and C — about 80% of all figures — and the remaining classes fall out of it or out of Option 4.

Option 3 — Construction script + geometry kernel

Powerful, later

What it is

  • Describe the figure the way the stem does: “triangle ABC with ∠A = α; drop the height from B to H on AC; bisect ∠B meeting AC at L”
  • A small 2D kernel executes it and returns real coordinates; the same kernel can derive the answer

For

  • Maximum generality — new figures without touching the renderer
  • The figure is provably consistent with the stated numbers, so it can be drawn to scale
  • Answer derivation comes free, which kills a whole class of wrong-key bugs

Against

  • Meaningfully more engineering: degenerate cases, intersection failures, orientation choices
  • Solves a problem the exam explicitly says you don't have — figures needn't be to scale
Phase 2. Build it behind the same scene-spec output, so it becomes an alternative producer of scenes rather than a second renderer.

Option 4 — Static asset library with label slots

For classes E and F

What it is

  • ~30 fixed SVG illustrations: solids, spinners, road schematics, a pole and cable
  • Each exposes named text slots; the item supplies the dimension strings

For

  • These figures carry no varying mathematical structure — only the labels change
  • Cheap, immediate, and visually identical to the real papers

Against

  • Repetition becomes visible if a student sits many papers; mitigate by holding several drawings per solid
Do this first — it's a day of work and unblocks the one 3D item every paper needs.

Rejected — an LLM emitting SVG at request time

Don't

Why not

  • Non-deterministic output for an artefact that must be identical for every student sitting the same paper
  • No way to validate that the drawn figure matches the stem, which is exactly the failure that makes an exam worthless
  • Visual inconsistency paper to paper; latency and cost on every render

Where a model does belong

  • Authoring time: propose a scene spec for a new template, which a human reviews once and commits
  • QA: read a rendered figure back and check it against the stem, as a gate — never as the producer
The existing nvo-math-verifier and nvo-format-guard agents are the right shape for this — verification, not generation.
Architecture · the rebuild

From 23 frozen slots to unlimited papers

The unit to author stops being a question and becomes an item template: a parameter space plus the functions that turn a sample of it into a stem, a key, distractors and a figure.

The item template

One file per template, roughly forty to sixty of them to cover every position in the matrix. This is the 2026 Q20 bisector item as a template:

# templates/geometry/incentre_angle.py
slot_topics   = ["geometry_triangle_bisector_diagram"]
positions     = {"mc": [11,12,13,14], "short": [19,20]}
points        = {"mc": 3, "short": 4}

def sample(rng):
    gamma = rng.choice([30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80])   # the angle to be found
    return {"gamma": gamma, "aob": 90 + gamma // 2}

def constraints(p):
    return p["aob"] % 1 == 0 and 100 <= p["aob"] <= 140   # plausible on paper

def stem(p):
    return f"Ъглополовящите на ∡BAC и ∡ABC се пресичат в точка O, "
           f"като ∡AOB = {p['aob']}°. Намерете мярката на ∡ACB."

def answer(p):        return f"{p['gamma']}°"

def distractors(p):   # see the grammar below
    return [supplement(p["gamma"]), half(p["gamma"]), p["aob"] - 90]

def scene(p):
    return triangle_with_cevians(
        apex_angle = p["gamma"],
        cevians    = [bisector("A", to="BC"), bisector("B", to="AC")],
        mark       = angle_at("O", between=("A","B"), label=f"{p['aob']}°"),
        arcs       = [double_arc_at("A"), double_arc_at("B")],
    )

The distractor grammar

The wrong options in these papers are not random — they are the specific mistakes a 7th-grader makes. Every distractor in the corpus falls into one of eight families. Encoding them is what makes a generated paper feel authentic rather than synthetic.

FamilyThe mistake it catchesSeen in
Sign flipDropped a minus somewhere in the chain2026 Q1 (−1,8 vs the positive), 2021 Q3
Arrested computationStopped one step early — the intermediate value is an option2026 Q7 (0,4% before the ×10), 2024 Q17
Bracket permutationWrong strictness or wrong direction on an interval2026 Q4, 2023 Q5, 2022 Q5, 2021 Q5 — all four options are the four variants
Supplement / complementGave 180° − x or 90° − x instead of x2023 Q11, 2022 Q11, 2024 Q10
Wrong angle in the figureRead a different marked angle off the drawing2026 Q11 (82° is ∠ABС + 42), 2021 Q10
Reciprocal or inverted fractionFlipped a ratio or a probability2024 Q20 (n/(n−10) vs (n−10)/n), 2026 Q6
Factor-sign permutationRight factors, wrong signs inside the brackets2025 Q3, 2024 Q3, 2022 Q3, 2019 Q4
Off-by-a-factorDoubled, halved, or forgot a unit conversion2022 Q18 (30 vs 300 vs 3000), 2021 Q18

The verifier gate

  • Uniqueness. Re-solve the item independently — symbolically with sympy where possible — and confirm the key matches and no distractor also satisfies the stem.
  • Distractor plausibility. Reject any option that is negative where a length is asked, non-integer where the others are integers, or an obvious outlier in magnitude — these give the answer away.
  • Figure agreement. Every named point in the stem exists in the scene; every value labelled on the figure appears in the parameter object.
  • Paper-level balance. Answer letters within ±2 of uniform; the points vector sums to exactly 65; algebra-to-geometry near 1 : 1 by the ministry's own memo; no topic repeated within a paper; no two items sharing a parameter sample.
  • Language. Cyrillic А/Б/В/Г not Latin A/B/C/D, correct mathematical notation (∡, △, cm, лв., °), no leftover template placeholders.
Decisions · taken and built

How each question was resolved

The generator described above now exists in the repo. These were the four judgement calls and what each became.

Which format does the product target?
2026 is the newest real paper and differs structurally from the 23-slot catalog in the repo. But students sitting the exam this year have been drilling on 2024–25 past papers.
BuiltBoth. The blueprint is a data file with one entry per era; GET /nvo/blueprints feeds a picker offering НВО 2026 (24 slots, 90 + 90 min) and Класически НВО (23 slots, 75 + 90 min). Item templates are shared — only the assembly differs. A third era costs one dict entry.
Do we generate Part 2, or curate it?
Part 2 items are 11–12 point multi-part proofs with a human-graded marking scheme. Generating them is a much harder problem than Part 1, and grading them automatically is harder still.
BuiltPart 1 is generated from 60 item templates; Part 2 is a curated bank of hand-authored items, each carrying its own marking scheme. Nine to start, extensible by adding a function. A student gets an unlimited supply of Part 1 and a deep but finite Part 2.
Is the formula sheet in scope?
The MON memo commits to issuing a свитък с формули as part of the exam packet, and it is not among the PDFs in NVOS/.
DeferredLeft out — the official sheet is not in the corpus, so there is nothing faithful to render. Still worth sourcing: a practice paper without it feels wrong, and it is a side panel, not a rebuild.
How much partial credit do we model?
The real keys award fractional credit in detail — “2 т., при един верен отговор”, “3 т., ако е написано 4x и 5x”. Multiple choice is all-or-nothing; short answer is not.
BuiltSlot.points is a tuple from day one, not an int, and reaches the client as NVOQuestion.points. A two-part short-answer item is (2, 3), and the verifier checks the sum against the blueprint.
Corpus — 13 PDFs in NVOS/, 148 pages, rendered at 130 dpi and read individually. Years covered: 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026, plus the MON format-change memo.
Variants — the corpus mixes Вариант 1, 2 and 3. Structure and point totals are identical across variants of the same year; only the numbers move.
Not yet covered — the official answer-sheet layout (лист за отговори), the formula sheet, and the Part 2 marking schemes beyond point totals.
